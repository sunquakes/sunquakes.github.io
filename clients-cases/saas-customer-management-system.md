# SaaS Customer Management System

A CRM is only as good as the customer data salespeople actually enter. This system is built around each rep's personal customer pool — with a competitive claiming mechanism that settles who owns a customer, incentives that reward registering new customers, and financial management that keeps orders, payments and receivables on the same record.

## The challenge

- **Every rep runs a personal book.** Each salesperson owns and works their own customers, and the system — not team etiquette — has to define who may touch whose customer.
- **Two reps, one customer.** When the same customer is approached by two people, there has to be a rule: who claimed first, how long that claim holds, and what happens when a customer goes quiet.
- **Data that never gets entered.** Sales staff keep customer knowledge in their heads or private notebooks unless registering a customer actually pays off for them.
- **Sales and finance drift apart.** Orders, payments, receivables and refunds live in different places, so nobody can see a customer's real balance without asking the finance team.

## Result

- Customer ownership is settled by the claim record — reps compete for open customers instead of fighting over whose customer it is.
- Customers actually get entered, because entering them earns something — the CRM became the team's record of truth rather than a side tool.
- Sales and finance read the same data: a customer's balance, payment history and receivables are one click from their record, so overdue payments surface before they age.
